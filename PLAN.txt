=====================================================================
1. WHAT WE ARE BUILDING (plain words)
=====================================================================
A city planner opens a dark map of Kelantan, moves it to the area to design, and LAJU:
  - decides where the bus stops go, from real population and destinations, and only where a bus can
    safely stop (big roads yes; expressways, ramps, roundabouts, bridges no; small roads are not used)
  - designs bus routes along real roads (baseline, genetic algorithm, deep RL, or RL + GA)
  - simulates passengers (trip time, transfers, unserved trips, bus loads) and traffic (SUMO)
  - exports GeoJSON (QGIS), GTFS and a printable report. Every run is saved on the planner's computer.
Buses only (no trams or trains). Kelantan only (the map cannot leave the state).

Customer: city planners. Goal: a working product + algorithm worth patenting / licensing as IP.

How the AI places stops and builds the network (kelantan.build):
  1. Key stops: weighted k-means over 400 m population hexagons plus places (hospital, clinic, school,
     university, market, mall, bus station, weighted by the trips they attract). Planner stops are fixed.
  2. Each key stop moves to the nearest OSM road node where a bus may stop: mid-block on a trunk /
     primary / secondary / tertiary road, >= 30 m from junctions, roundabouts, bridges, tunnels, ramps.
     The road classes live in ONE table (kelantan.ROADS): speed + "may stop here".
  3. Links: key stops whose road catchments touch (plus two-hop neighbours) are joined by the quickest
     road path that passes no other key stop. Turning back at a key stop is a U-turn (sim.UTURN_MIN).
  4. Intermediate stops along each link: every 400 m (dense) / 800 m / 1.5 km (rural), only on
     stop-able road with >= 50 people nearby; links on the same road share stops.
  5. Trips (since 2026-10-29, brain v2 Phase 1): person trips by every mode and purpose (kelantan.PURPOSES: work,
     school, study, health, shop, leisure, travel, other; 2.8 a person a day) between 2,000 demand zones for the whole
     state (kelantan.zones, cached), each purpose's gravity beta fitted to its mean trip minutes. Places pull their
     purpose (kelantan.KINDS) by size: a hospital by its district's beds, a school by its district's pupils, a mall,
     supermarket, market or office by its building's footprint in OSM (kelantan.TRIPS_PER_M2); jobs on OSM's
     commercial / retail / industrial land and at the places; part of work, shopping and leisure stays near home
     (kelantan.NEAR_HOME). Summed between the key stops in view: a trip inside one key stop's area is local.
     kelantan.BUS_SHARE (2.3%) of them is the bus's potential until Phase 2. (Before: residents made 0.05 bus trips a
     day, spread over the other key stops by attraction x exp(-0.05 x min).)
Score v3 (sim._v3; Kelantan since 2026-10-29, brain v2 Phase 2): riders choose. Every trip between key stops compares
  the bus (waits x 2, walks x 2, 10 min per change, plus kelantan.BUS_ASC = 315 felt min) with a motorbike or car (0.8x the
  bus's road minutes + 2 min each end), or, for people without one (kelantan.CAPTIVE, by purpose), a lift that feels
  120 min worse (sim.LIFT_MIN), by a logit (sim.THETA = 0.015 per felt minute). BUS_ASC makes BAS.MY's own 16 routes at
  their timetable carry the 2,800 boardings a day BAS.MY reports. The routes share the planner's buses (max_buses, else
  kelantan.BUSES = 64) in whole buses: each route what its minimum service needs, every further bus where it cuts most
  waiting (sim._share, square-root rule); two rounds (minimum service, then the shared-out buses). Score = 1000 /
  (riders + kelantan.COVER_WEIGHT x people within 400 m town / 800 m rural of a stop with a bus), x the buses the minimum
  services need beyond the budget, routes over the time cap and crooked minutes; lower is better. COVER_WEIGHT 0.03 =
  "coverage first", the planner's pick. RM 100 per unserved trip, RM 10/h and RM per bus-km are gone from it.
  By period of the day (since 2026-10-29 evening, brain v2 Phase 3): 4 periods (kelantan.PERIODS: morning peak 6-9, day
  9-16, evening peak 16-19, night 19-24; 18 h of service), each with its own trips (kelantan.demand: a purpose's out trips
  by its out shares, the trips back by its back shares). Round 1 puts every period's trips on the paths at the minimum
  service: each route's riders and busiest-hour load (sim.PEAK_HOUR 1.3x the period's average hour) per period. Each route
  then runs a clock-face headway per period (sim.CLOCK: 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 60, 120 min, so buses can meet), never over its
  minimum service, never over-full; the step to a shorter headway that saves most waiting per extra bus goes first while
  the period's buses fit the budget (sim._share), and a period with buses to spare buys only steps worth as much as the
  last one the budget allowed in the period that ran out (one price of a bus all day: quiet hours run fewer buses,
  "jimat"). Round 2 runs each period at its headways. A change at a hub waits sim.TIMED_MIN = 5 min, not half the
  headway (timed transfers): a key stop within 300 m of a bus or rail station where 2+ routes meet, or any stop where 3+
  meet (sim.HUB_ROUTES). Routes end only where a bus can turn, wait and its driver rest (kelantan.rests: bus and rail
  stations, malls, hospitals, universities, colleges, stadiums, roundabouts on bus roads, car parks of 2,500 m2+; 227
  places): every bus drives on empty from its route's ends to the nearest one, waits there and comes back each round trip
  (kelantan.bases, Problem.rest_min: more buses for the same headway), and a route end more than a 5-min drive from one
  (kelantan.REST_RUN = 10 min there and back, Problem.rest_end) multiplies the score by 1.1 (sim.REST_END). The GA's new
  routes run between such stops, and a route grows to one along the quickest road or shrinks back to one
  (routes.settle). Reported, not scored: each bus's run from the nearest bus station
  (the depots: 19) and back a day (empty_km_day), and the routes an electric bus can run on 250 km plus 2 km a minute of
  charging in its layovers (sim.electric). BAS.MY at its official timetable (exam.basmy: data.gov.my GTFS; headways per
  period from its real departures, exam.periods) times no transfers and runs no express; kelantan.BUS_ASC 323.3 makes it
  carry its 2,800 boardings a day on the same 150 key stops as LAJU's designs (since 2026-10-31; 315 and 301.1 before, on
  traced routes).
Score v2 (sim.py; Mandl and the synthetic RL cities still use it) in RM per trip = (RM 10/h x passenger time incl. walking, waiting and a transfer penalty (Kelantan
  10 min since 2026-10-10, Mandl 5) + bus-km x RM per bus-km of the route's category + RM 100 per unserved trip (50 until
  2026-10-18; a trip needing 3+ changes counts as unserved since 2026-10-18, kelantan.MAX_TRANSFERS)) / trips.
  Budgets (max buses, max km), routes over the time cap and crooked routes (over 1.5x the quickest drive between their
  two ends, since 2026-10-10; +1/600 per minute since 2026-10-18, sim.CROOKED_MIN) multiply it. Walking counts homes and, since 2026-10-10, the doors of the places trips go
  to. All knobs sit at the top of kelantan.py and sim.py: they are assumptions, not survey data.
Bus categories (sim.CATEGORIES, since 2026-10-08): a route's length and stops pick its category.
  shuttle    under 20 km and 25 stops   minibus 30 pax, RM 2.5/bus-km, minimum service (planner) default 30 min
  local      up to 40 km and 65 stops   city bus 60 pax, RM 4.0/bus-km, default 30 min
  inter-town longer                     coach 45 pax,    RM 3.5/bus-km, default 60 min
  (defaults 10 / 15 / 30 until 2026-10-18). Each route runs at the minimum service or more often: as its busiest hour
  needs (peak load / bus capacity, Baaj & Mahmassani 1991) or as its riders' waiting is worth (square-root rule,
  Mohring 1972: f = sqrt(30 x boardings x RM per minute / (2 x route km x service hours x RM per bus-km))), and its
  riders wait half the headway it really runs (the routing assumes the minimum service). Kelantan scores from before
  a rule change are not comparable with later ones.

How the deep-RL designer works (rl.py, rewritten 2026-10-08 evening):
  1. Decisions: where a route starts, which stop it heads for next (the bus takes the quickest road path
     there, so one decision can cross the map), or that the route ends. Masked: max stops, route-time cap,
     no U-turn where two legs meet, never back through the route's own stops.
  2. Network: a 6-layer encoder (width 128) passes messages along road links AND along trips (who travels
     where), once per map; a 1-layer decoder reads the design so far at every decision (covered stops,
     each stop's trips with no direct route yet, the trips a leg would make direct, leg minutes and stops).
     No per-size weights: the same network runs on 15 or 150 key stops.
  3. Training (python rl.py [minutes]): REINFORCE with a shared baseline (Kool 2019, POMO 2020): 16 designs
     per map, each scored by sim.evaluate (the same RM score the planner sees), pushed up or down by how much
     better than the 16's mean (log score). Maps: 300 random Kelantan map views (15-150 key stops, routes,
     headways and caps varied) + 20% synthetic minutes-score cities. 4 worker processes update one shared
     network (Hogwild). Saved when it beats the saved policy on 9 held-out maps (best of 8 samples).
  4. Design time: best of 32 samples (1-4 s), then active search (Bello 2017, Hottung 2022): the policy keeps
     training on the planner's own map for the search time. "RL + GA": the GA starts from 8 policy samples. "Train"
     (server._train) adds 16 new policy samples to the GA's population at the start and every 10 min (AI_EVERY_S), so a
     saved network takes in what the policy learns while python rl.py trains (since 2026-10-18).
  5. Live (since 2026-10-18): while python rl.py trains, its main process draws the policy's design of the saved
     "Kelantan network" (rl.showcase / rl.frame: best of 8 with a fixed seed, so the lines move only when the policy
     changed) every ~15 s into the temp folder (server.RL_LIVE); the website's AI training tab shows it on the map.

C++: SUMO (Eclipse SUMO, C++) runs as a binary from Python (pip package eclipse-sumo). We only write our
own C++ if profiling shows a Python hotspot numpy/scipy cannot fix (none so far: one evaluation ~1 ms).

3. HOW TO RUN (two terminals)
=====================================================================
  Terminal 1 (engine):
    cd engine
    python -m venv .venv                                   (first time only; Python 3.14)
    .venv\Scripts\pip install -r requirements.txt          (first time only)
    .venv\Scripts\python kelantan.py                       (first time only: ~300 MB download, ~5 min)
    .venv\Scripts\python -m uvicorn server:app --port 8000 --reload

  Terminal 2 (web):
    cd web
    npm install                                            (first time only)
    npm run dev
    open http://localhost:3000

  Self-checks:   python sim.py | routes.py | rl.py | export.py | kelantan.py | traffic.py | exam.py   -> "ok ..."
  The exam:      python exam.py "BAS.MY today" "Kelantan network"   (saved runs side by side, ~2 min; python exam.py
                 --basmy saves BAS.MY's official timetable as the run "BAS.MY today": it fetches the GTFS into
                 cache/basmy_gtfs.zip, delete that file to fetch it again)
  GPS log:       python gps.py   (in its own window, "LAJU GPS logger", started 2026-10-31 20:27; stdlib only, ~0% CPU;
                 every 30 s each bus's new position into engine/cache/gps/<date>.csv; close the window to stop, run it
                 again to resume; a sleeping laptop leaves gaps; python gps.py --test = self-check)
  Benchmarks:    python bench.py [ga_seconds]
  Train RL more: python rl.py <minutes> [workers]   (minutes the laptop is awake: sleep does not count, since a
                 2026-10-09 run slept 4 times and used up its clock time asleep; continues from models/route_gnn.pt at lr 1e-4; 4 processes
                 by default, all 8 cores, ~4 GB RAM; 2 workers ~2 GB, for a busy 16 GB laptop: a 4-worker run
                 was stopped for low memory on 2026-10-08; the first run builds cache/rl_cities.pkl, ~10 min;
                 replaces models/route_gnn.pt only when better; live in the website's AI training tab: its curve and
                 its own design of the Kelantan network on the map. Delete cache/rl_cities.pkl whenever kelantan.build
                 or the Problem fields change, e.g. a knob at the top of kelantan.py; sim.evaluate changes do not need it)

  The project sits in OneDrive: file watching is unreliable there. If a change does not show up,
  restart `npm run dev` / uvicorn.

5. DECISIONS (and why)
=====================================================================
- Baseline heuristic first, deep RL later: RL needs the road graph, demand data and simulator as its
  environment anyway (Phases 2-3).
- "Train" in the UI = the GA keeps searching the planner's own map, starting from the design it has (by
  default an RL + GA design). The curve shows the best cost per improvement; "flat for > half the
  training time" is the hint that more time will not help. Untested: active search or the policy
  mutation inside Train on long runs (they only tie RL + GA at 30 s).
- Deep RL rewrite (2026-10-08 evening). The first policy picked one adjacent stop at a time, saw no
  travel times and no trip pairs, and trained only on small synthetic cities with a minutes score; on
  Kelantan it lost to the GA everywhere and, on big maps, to the baseline. Changes, each for a reason:
  heading for any stop along the quickest path (a route is 2-4 decisions, not 15, and can aim far);
  messages along trips as well as roads (3 road hops cannot see where people go); training on real
  Kelantan map views with the real score (what the product runs on); REINFORCE with a shared baseline
  instead of PPO (no value network, no padding to a fixed size, the standard for routing problems);
  4 Hogwild processes (8 threads in one process were only 2x one thread). This dropped Gymnasium,
  Stable-Baselines3 and sb3-contrib from requirements.txt.
- RL validation = best of 8 samples on 9 held-out maps: greedy alone swung 0.72-0.83 between checks.
  Training never overwrites a policy that scores better on that set, so a fresh run cannot make the
  website worse. Held-out maps are random Kelantan views, so they overlap the training views in space.
- Active search learning rate 1e-3: at 1e-4 it barely moved in 90 s on Kota Bharu; at 1e-3 the score
  went 36 -> 19 RM/trip from an untrained policy.
- Longest-route cap: without it the score prefers 5-hour routes on state-wide designs, because an
  unserved trip costs RM 50 and the gravity model sends trips across the whole state (see section 7).
- Python for AI/simulation (PyTorch, SUMO, scipy all live there).
- Save locally as JSON for now; database only if multi-user is needed.
- Engine server and Next.js are separate processes so long jobs do not freeze the website.
- OSM from the Geofabrik extract (read locally with GDAL, via geopandas) instead of osmnx/Overpass:
  on 2026-10-08 both overpass-api.de servers timed out from this network. The extract is one 250 MB
  download and also gives the places and district boundaries.
- Two levels of stops: key stops (40-150) are what the GA/RL design over; intermediate stops give
  walking access. Keeps the optimisation small while stops end up 400 m-1.5 km apart.
- Road classes decide everything about stops in one table (kelantan.ROADS). Small roads are excluded
  for buses entirely; expressways carry buses but never stops.
- Unserved trips cost RM 100 (soft constraint; RM 50 until 2026-10-18). An "e-hailing fare" price made serving people
  and abandoning them cost about the same, and the GA then designed near-empty networks. At RM 50, on the final rules of
  2026-10-10 (transfers 10 min, walking to the door), Move stops saved 0.13% by dropping 16% of the state's trips, and the
  score tied a state network leaving 20.3% of trips without a bus with one leaving 8.5% (33.72 vs 33.77). Serving the area
  is the goal, so RM 100 (43.94 vs 38.08).
- RL training cities: up to 60 routes and 2.5 stops per route (was 30 routes, 4 stops per route): the Kelantan network has
  150 key stops and 60 routes, a setting the policy had never practised (2026-10-18). Synthetic cities' connecting tree is
  scipy's minimum_spanning_tree (same tree as the hand-written Prim it replaced).
- U-turns: key stops are mid-block, so a route that leaves a stop by the road it came in on must loop
  round (sim.UTURN_MIN = 10 min). Generators avoid them; SUMO shows the loops that remain.
- Bus category is a property of the route (length, stops), not a separate gene: the categories are
  defined by length and stops, so the GA picks a category by picking the route. Bus size reaches the
  score through the extra buses a busy route needs, so minibuses win on quiet feeders, city buses on
  busy corridors, coaches on long thin ones. Per-route frequency = minimum service, what the peak needs or what its
  riders' waiting is worth, held all day (ponytail: peak/off-peak timetables would cut off-peak bus-km).
- Fewer buses, same coverage (planner, 2026-10-18: "lesser bus more jimat" but "cover semua"): quiet routes keep a
  minimum service (30 min in town, 60 between towns, was 10 / 15 / 30) and busy ones run as often as their riders need.
  The saved state network: 486 buses at the old minimum, 367 at the new, average trip 94 -> 101 min (waiting), RM 45.66
  -> 42.88 per trip; 30 / 60 / 60 saved 16 buses more but locals hourly is poor town service. Fewer buses does not need
  fewer routes or slower routes: the routes stay, the quiet ones come less often.
- 3+ changes = no bus (planner, 2026-10-18): riders do not make such trips (12% of the state's trips needed them);
  the UTRP literature reports them as d_un. A trip whose quickest path needs more than kelantan.MAX_TRANSFERS (2) changes
  is taken off before the loads are summed (sim._transfers_and_loads), so it costs RM 100 and rides no bus. ponytail: a
  slower path with fewer changes is not searched; the 10-min transfer penalty already prefers it when close.
- Walking is part of passenger time (kelantan.build step 5, sim.Problem.walk): each trip end walks
  from home to the key stop, or to the nearest stop on a link that ends there while a bus stops on that
  link. 17 min/km, capped at 15 min (beyond, people ride a motorbike or get dropped off; 30 until
  2026-10-09), and each walking minute weighs double in the score (heat: WALK_WEIGHT). Precomputed
  per zone and per link, so an evaluation adds a matrix sum, not a per-home search. Each home counts
  only its nearest link stop (ponytail: serving a second nearby link saves it nothing more).
- Route shape (2026-10-10, planner: "our routes look bad"): 12 of the 60 state-network routes took over 1.5x the
  quickest drive between their two ends (route 32: 80 min for a 23-min drive): hooks into side roads, zigzags, small
  loops. Nothing in the score cared. sim.Problem.max_detour (Kelantan 1.5, kelantan.MAX_DETOUR): the minutes beyond
  1.5x, averaged over the routes, multiply the score by 1 + minutes/60. In the score, not in the generators, so
  baseline, GA, RL and Move stops all obey it; the RL learns it from the reward (no mask: a route that looks crooked
  halfway can end straight). First version (02:35-03:25) summed the minutes over all routes: on the 60-route network
  that multiplied the score by 6.8, more than RM 50 per unserved trip is worth, so the GA dropped riders to straighten
  routes (no bus 2.4% -> 16.5%) and the RL learned tiny routes (no bus 11% -> 79% on its test maps). Averaged, a crooked
  route still costs more than it saves, but dropping riders never pays (Kota Bharu, GA 30 s: 7 of 8 routes within
  1.5x, no bus 3.9%). A penalty that multiplies the whole score must stay small next to the unserved cost.
  2026-10-18 (planner: stronger): averaged over 60 routes, a crooked minute cost 1/3600 of the score, so the state
  network kept 8 crooked routes; now 1/600 per minute however many routes (the 8-route designs had 1/480 and kept their
  riders). 1/300 was tried first: x1.29 on the state network, as risky as the first rule.
- Busy places (2026-10-10, planner: "high-demand places don't get many routes"): on the state network the 15 busiest key
  stops (22% of trip ends) mostly had 1 route (all stops: 1.6; demand vs routes correlation 0.26) and 31 of 130 big
  places (hospitals, malls, universities, markets, terminals) had no route within 500 m. Two causes, two changes:
  (1) only homes walked: a trip ending at a hospital was charged its zone's residents' walk, so a stop 1 km from the
  hospital cost nothing, and stop spots and Move stops chased homes only. Now each place's visitors walk from its door
  (kelantan.build walkers wP/wpop, also net["wP"] for Move stops; coverage still counts residents): a place counts as
  plw * qbar / (TRIP_RATE * (1 + qbar)) residents, qbar = trips per unit of attraction, because a resident starts
  TRIP_RATE trips and ends TRIP_RATE * qbar while a place ends plw * qbar. Places are ~40% of trip ends in Kota Bharu.
  (2) A transfer counted 5 min (Mandl's convention), so changing buses was nearly free and the AI spread routes thin;
  Kelantan now 10 (kelantan.TRANSFER_MIN; studies use 10-15). Test in section 7: every measure improved.
- Shops by size (2026-10-18, planner: "fix the aeon mall thing"): every mall attracted 2,500 trips a day, so AEON Mall
  Kota Bharu (32,000 m2, the state's biggest) counted as much as Bazar Tuan Padang (1,500 m2); its key stop ended 568
  trips a day and had 1 route, and SK Kota -> AEON needed 2 changes. Now a mall, supermarket or market attracts trips by
  the footprint of the building OSM maps it in (places.json "m2"), at rates that keep each kind's old total over the
  sized places (0.24 / 0.13 / 0.75 trips per m2; 9 of 11 malls, 36 of 73 supermarkets, 22 of 37 markets are sized; the
  rest keep PLACES). AEON 7,684 trips, KB Mall 5,105, Mydin 3,237, Pasar Besar Siti Khadijah 3,968. Buildings only:
  markets mapped as open lots (Pasar Awam Bukit Bunga, 15,000 m2) would outdraw every hospital. Footprint, not floor
  area (building:levels is on 1 in 6 retail buildings): a 4-storey bazaar counts its ground floor. The state network's
  key stops are pinned (input.spots), so its routes carried over; AEON's key stop now ends 1,683 trips a day, Mydin's
  1,883 (1 route, 30% no bus): Train resumed on the new demand at 10:49 to fix both.
- Express inter-town, per link: key stops only where another bus still stops (free), or where its
  riders' saved dwell beats the extra walking of the people near the skipped stops. Decided greedily
  after the passenger assignment (ponytail: riders who would switch to the faster bus are not counted;
  a second assignment pass would, at twice the cost). No express gene: given the routes, the best
  express pattern is a local decision, so the GA only shapes the routes.
- SUMO network is built from the same OSM graph (plain XML -> netconvert) and buses follow the
  planned roads, so plan and simulation compare like with like. Batch runs, so no TraCI.
- PDF report = browser print, no PDF library.
- Map: satellite by default (Esri World Imagery + Esri place names, 2026-10-08 evening, planner's request);

9. BRAIN v2 (planned 2026-10-28; Phase 0 done the same day, Phases 1-3 on 2026-10-29, Phase 4 next)

Why. The planner wants "the best AI public transport planning": Kelantan as dependent on public transport as Japan, not
car-centric. Measured on the saved "Kelantan network" (v1: 382 buses, 60 routes) on 2026-10-28, the old brain optimised
the wrong thing well:
  - Fake long trips. Demand used only the 150 key-stop zones for the whole state and threw away trips inside a zone, so
    63% of trips were longer than a 30-min drive (average 44 min). Real bus trips are mostly short and in town.
  - Slow for riders: 102 min door to door on average (19 of them walking) vs 44 min driving the same roads. Direct 25%,
    1 change 45%, 2 changes 23%, no bus 6.5%.
  - "Best" was one RM number made of guesses (RM 100 per unserved trip, RM 10/h, RM 2.5-4 per bus-km); everyone counted
    as riding however bad the bus; 382 buses where Kelantan really runs 64.
  - People within 400 m (town) / 800 m (rural) of a served stop: town 45.8%, rural 27.0%, all 38.4%.
Real anchors (public reports, found 2026-10-28):
  - BAS.MY Kelantan (Konsortium E-Mutiara, official launch 29 Jan 2026, Bernama): 16 routes D10-D62, 64 buses (8
    electric midi, 24 low-floor mini, 32 mini), RM 30 monthly pass; ~84,000 passengers a month in Q4 2025 (~2,800 a
    day; one source, carz.com.my). Route list with termini and main stops, no road paths:
    en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_BAS.MY_bus_routes.
  - Japan (MLIT, 2014): 5.8% of people live over 500 m from a bus stop and over 1 km from a station, so ~94% are
    covered. MLIT's guideline: a bus stop within 300-500 m, a station within 500-1,000 m.
  - Kelantan's ceiling for big buses (measured 2026-10-28): 60.7% of people live at >= 1,000 per km2 ("town"). Within
    400 m (town) / 800 m (rural) of a road a big bus may stop on (trunk..tertiary): town 78.5%, rural 71.7%, all 75.8%
    (81.7% at Japan's 500 m / 1 km). So 80-90% coverage needs vans on small roads (later), not more buses.

Planner's answers (2026-10-28), the rules from here on:

  - More frequent buses: long waits, far stops and the heat are why people avoid the bus. Main corridors every 15 min or
    better. Buses meet at hubs at the same minutes (timed transfers). At most 2 changes.
  - Service 6 am - 12 am (18 h), with peak / off-peak / evening frequencies ("jimat").

The exam: what "better" means from now on. The planner's dream: "go to school by bus, to the mall, the stadium, the
hospital". Every design is judged on the same simulator, the same people and the same number of buses:
  1. Access: the share of people who can reach, by bus with at most 2 changes, door to door (walk + wait + ride + waits
     at changes, real minutes): their nearest school within 30 min, a clinic or hospital within 45, a mall or market
     within 45, a rail station within 45, a park or stadium within 60, their district town within 45. Per district
     (fairness). The minutes are knobs.
  2. Coverage: people within 400 m (town) / 800 m (rural) of a stop a bus serves.
  3. Riders a day (from Phase 2), buses, bus-km, RM a year.
  4. Named trips: SK Kota -> KB Mall, SK Kota -> AEON Mall, Wakaf Che Yeh -> HRPZ II.
  5. Shape: routes over 1.5x their quickest drive; every route ends at a rest place.
  6. Benchmarks: Mandl (have) and Mumford's 4 cities (small public files), standard UTRP evaluation, vs published results.
"Beats BAS.MY" = more people pass 1 and 2, and more riders, with the same 64 buses. Before its rider numbers are trusted,

Eyes and experience (planned 2026-10-31; not built, no code until the planner says go). The planner: "make the AI as if
it has eyes and can experience the road". Why: the AI sees averages on a map (every wait half the headway, one speed per
road class, every person alike, guessed knobs). Riders live through noon heat, waits without shade, late buses and missed
changes, and decide by their bad days, not their average ones. Four parts:
  1. Eyes: see the real place.
     - BAS.MY's own data on data.gov.my (found 2026-10-31; the static file was empty on 2026-10-28):
       api.data.gov.my/gtfs-static/mybas-kota-bharu = the official timetable (Konsortium E-Mutiara: 16 routes D10-D62,
       755 stops with their positions, 376 trips, stop times per stop, route shapes, weekday and weekend services, first
       bus 06:30, last 19:00; trips per route match basmy.json). api.data.gov.my/gtfs-realtime/vehicle-position/
       mybas-kota-bharu = live bus GPS (38 buses reporting at 19:21 on Saturday 31 Oct, each with its plate and trip).
       Logged every 30 s for weeks: real speeds by hour and road, delays against the timetable, buses really in service,
       how evenly they come. No passenger counts in either feed.
     - Shade: ESA WorldCover tree cover (10 m, free, CC BY 4.0), so a walk to the stop counts its heat, not just metres.
     - Street photos: Mapillary (open; Kelantan coverage to check). A vision AI rates each key stop site: sidewalk,
       shade, a safe place for the bus to pull in.
  2. Experience: live a day. People one by one instead of averages (a synthetic Kelantan: each person a home, a job or
     school, an age, a motorbike or not). Each lives a day: heat by the hour and shade on the walk, traffic delays (from
     the GPS log), crowded buses, missed changes, rain. They remember: bad days make them quit, so riders = who still
     rides after a month. Personas: Mak Cik Aminah (68, no car) to HRPZ II; a Form 4 pupil to school; a foreign worker to a
     factory at 7 am; the exam's 3 test trips become hundreds. Tool: MATSim (open source, used by many cities) or a
     lighter Python version; a 10% sample on the laptop.
  3. Thinking: plan like a senior planner.
     - Layers: main corridors and rail first, then hubs, then feeders, then timetables (now all at once).
     - Learn from its own best: the search's best designs train the AI, which then proposes better ones (expert
       iteration, as AlphaZero learned).
     - Gut feeling: a fast copy of the slow day simulation screens millions of designs; the full simulation checks the
       best few.
     - Robust: every design tried under what-ifs (riders more or less sensitive: THETA, BUS_ASC; more traffic; a rain
       day); pick one good in all of them, not perfect for one guess.
     - Planner's eye: a critic that checks the map the way the planner does (zigzags, busy places missed, route ends)
       and explains each route (Phase 4).
  4. Reality loop: after launch, real ridership and GPS recalibrate the model; the master network changes a little at a
     time.
  Limits: laptop CPU, no money for compute: samples, vision only at the key stops. Ridership per route is still missing:
  ask Konsortium E-Mutiara (e-mutiara.com.my) or APAD with a UKM letter, or count riders by hand on a few trips.
  Order: (1) the official timetable as "BAS.MY today" (replaces the traced basmy.json) and start the GPS log; (2) the
  persona day simulation with delays and heat; (3) layers and learn-from-best in Phase 5; (4) the what-ifs; (5) street
  photos. Without real data even the best thinking optimises a guess.
